01 — Definition

A simulator that lives inside the mind

A world model is a learned model of how an environment works. Given the current state and an action, it predicts the next state. Because it can roll that prediction forward many steps, an agent can try things out in imagination before it does them for real.

If an organism carries a small-scale model of reality in its head, it can try out alternatives before acting.Paraphrasing Kenneth Craik, The Nature of Explanation, 1943
perceiveEncodeCompress pixels and sensors into a compact state z
predictDynamicsLearn p(zt+1 | zt, at), the "physics"
planImagineRoll out futures, score them, pick the best action
Awake: the model watches the world, compresses it, predicts the next state and acts.

03 — Why now

Language got us far. Physics is the next frontier.

Large language models learned the world through text. But robots, cars and agents have to act in the world, and text alone doesn't teach gravity, occlusion or what happens after you push something.

LLM · models text

  • Predicts the next token
  • Learns from the internet's text (trillions of tokens)
  • Great at reasoning in language, code, knowledge
  • Weak at intuitive physics, 3D space, cause and effect over time

World model · models dynamics

  • Predicts the next state of the world, given an action
  • Learns from video, sensors, simulation and interaction
  • Great at planning, counterfactuals, generating experience
  • Weak at long horizons, exact physics, cost (for now)
01 / DATA

Robots don't have an internet

Real robot data is scarce, slow and expensive to collect. World models can generate synthetic experience from video, at a scale no physical fleet could match.

Cosmos 1 was trained on ~20M hours of video
02 / EFFICIENCY

Imagination is cheap

Agents can learn inside their own "dreams" and need far fewer real trials. DreamerV3 collected diamonds in Minecraft from scratch with one set of hyperparameters.

Dreamer 4 (2025) did it from offline video only
03 / PLANNING

Think before you act

With a model, an agent can search over futures. MuZero mastered Go, chess, shogi and Atari without ever being told the rules.

Planning = search over imagined rollouts
04 / SAFETY

Rehearse the rare and the dangerous

A child running into the road or a tornado on the highway are hard to collect and impossible to stage. A world model can generate them on demand.

Waymo World Model (2026), built on Genie 3
05 / CAUSALITY

Counterfactuals: "what if?"

Correlation in text isn't causation in the world. An action-conditioned model can answer "what would have happened if I'd turned left?"

Key ingredient for scientific and causal AI
06 / AGI

The missing piece of intelligence

Animals plan with internal models. Many researchers, LeCun among them, argue that human-level AI needs predictive world models, not just bigger LLMs.

AMI Labs raised ~$1.03B for this bet (2026)
04 — Five schools of thought

There's no single recipe

"World model" covers a family of approaches. They differ in what they predict (pixels, latents, 3D geometry or rewards) and in what they're for.

What works today

  • Real-time, explorable worlds from a text or image prompt
  • Synthetic driving and robot data for training and testing
  • Learned weather models that beat physics-based forecasts
  • Agents that learn skills inside learned simulators
  • Persistent, exportable 3D scenes (splats and meshes)

Not solved yet

  • Consistency beyond minutes of interaction
  • Reliable, measurable physical accuracy
  • Cheap inference: real-time worlds still need large accelerators
  • Multi-agent, long-memory, editable worlds

08 — Robotics case study

Is Ridge Q1 a world model?

Ridge Q1 is a 12-joint robot dog with a 4-joint arm, specified down to its 618 parts. It runs in a simulated warehouse: lidar builds a costmap, A* plans routes, Claude Haiku turns commands into pick-and-place steps, and practice rounds turn failures into written lessons. It's a great robotics example. Let's test it against the definition from scene 02.

open full page ↗

Loads the full 3D simulation here (three.js). Its Claude-powered planning and practice need the page to run inside Claude; elsewhere it falls back to a keyword parser.

World-model checklist

✓
Keeps an internal state of the worldA 10 cm costmap built from simulated lidar. A crate you drop only appears once the lidar sees it.
✕
Predicts the next state, given an actionA* only asks "is this cell free, and what does it cost?". It never asks "what happens if I lift 1.2 kg?" The physics lives in the simulator, which is the robot's reality, not a model inside its head.
✕
Imagines outcomes before actingIt discovers the real 1.0 kg wrist limit, the 0.13 m slip and the two-item bin by trying and failing for real. A world model would have predicted those failures.
~
Learned from dataThe map is measured, not learned. The "lessons" are English rules Haiku writes after each round. That's useful, but it's a list of facts, not a model you can roll forward.
✓
Improves with experiencePractice scores go up as lessons build up. This is reflective learning, in the style of Reflexion.
2 of 5 · verdict: not a world modelRidge Q1 is a map-based planner with a language-model brain and a verbal memory. Its lessons are, at best, a symbolic sketch of a world model: they record what went wrong but can't simulate what will happen next. To become a world model it needs a learned p(outcome | state, action) that it uses to imagine before it acts. That's the robot on the next scene.
09 — The same robot, with a world model

Ridge W imagines the delivery first

Same warehouse, same hidden truths: the spec sheet says 1.5 kg, but the wrist trips above 1.0 kg, and the wet floor makes a loaded robot slip. 13 — Looking ahead

    
Where this is heading

    

      
LLM + world model fusion
Language for reasoning, a world model for physics. Vision-language-action models (Gemini Robotics, GR00T, π0) are the first step.

      
Persistent, editable worlds
Worlds that remember for hours, can be edited with prompts, and can be shared by many agents and people.

      
Benchmarks for physics
Agreed-upon tests of physical accuracy, so "looks right" becomes "measurably right".

      
Edge and on-device
Distilled models and splat renderers that run on phones, headsets and robots, not just in data centers.

      
Science simulators
Learned models of climate, materials, cells and cities that make "what-if" experiments cheap.
